# SOURCES — credential-anthropology (Anthropological / Ethnographic Professional Standards)

All rules in `class.yaml` derive from the AAA Code of Ethics, the SfAA Ethical Guidelines, UNESCO cultural heritage declarations, ICOMOS standards, the Geneva Convention IV (cultural property), and established anthropological principles. Every rule cites its specific source. No rule exists without a source.

## Primary standards

### AAA (American Anthropological Association) Code of Ethics
- **American Anthropological Association, AAA Statement on Ethics: Principles of Professional Responsibility (2012, reaffirmed 2020).**
  <https://www.americananthro.org/ethics/statement-on-ethics/>
- Key principles cited:
  - Principle I: Do No Harm — Primary ethical obligation; anticipate and avoid harm
  - Principle II: Be Open and Honest Regarding Your Work — Transparency, positionality, informed consent
  - Principle III: Obtain Informed Consent and Necessary Permissions — Ongoing process; community-level consent
  - Principle IV: Weigh Competing Ethical Obligations Due Collaborators and Affected Parties — Reciprocity, multiple stakeholders
  - Principle V: Make Your Results Accessible — Share findings with communities
  - Principle VI: Protect and Preserve Your Records — Data stewardship, long-term preservation

### SfAA (Society for Applied Anthropology) Ethical Guidelines
- **Society for Applied Anthropology (SfAA), Ethical and Professional Responsibilities (current edition).**
  <https://www.sfaa.net/about/ethics/>
- Key guidelines cited:
  - Responsibility to the people studied (primary obligation)
  - Responsibility to the community and broader society
  - Responsibility to the discipline, science, and profession
  - Responsibility to students, trainees, and clients
  - Professional responsibilities regarding public discourse

### UNESCO Universal Declaration on Cultural Diversity (2001)
- **UNESCO, Universal Declaration on Cultural Diversity, adopted November 2, 2001.**
  <https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000127160>
- Key articles cited:
  - Article 1 — Cultural diversity as common heritage of humanity
  - Article 4 — Human rights as guarantee of cultural diversity
  - Article 7 — Cultural heritage as the wellspring of creativity
  - Article 11 — Market forces must not threaten cultural diversity
  - Article 12 — Role of UNESCO in promoting cultural diversity

### ICOMOS Cultural Heritage Standards
- **International Council on Monuments and Sites (ICOMOS):**
  - International Cultural Tourism Charter (1999) — Managing Tourism at Places of Heritage Significance
  - Charter for the Protection and Management of the Archaeological Heritage (1990)
  - Principles for the Analysis, Conservation and Structural Restoration of Architectural Heritage (2003)
  - ICOMOS Ethical Commitment Statement (2020)
- <https://www.icomos.org/en/resources/charters-and-texts>

### Geneva Convention IV — Cultural Property
- **1954 Hague Convention for the Protection of Cultural Property in the Event of Armed Conflict (and its First and Second Protocols).**
  <https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000082464>
- Key provisions:
  - Article 1 — Definition of cultural property
  - Article 4 — Respect for cultural property; refraining from use for military purposes
  - Article 5 — Occupation and protection of cultural property
  - First Protocol (1954) — Export of cultural property from occupied territories
  - Second Protocol (1999) — Enhanced protection; criminal responsibility

## Coverage honesty

**Checkable rules (5):** `do-no-harm`, `informed-consent`, `cultural-relativism`, `no-cultural-appropriation`, `positionality`.

These are codifiable as regex patterns that detect specific text-based violations: stereotyping and harmful characterizations of communities, bypassing informed consent requirements, ethnocentric judgments about cultural practices, encouraging or supporting cultural appropriation, and claiming objective/neutral analysis without positionality acknowledgment. They are high precision but narrow — they detect specific known-bad text patterns but do not evaluate whether the AI's overall cultural analysis is sound.

**Teaching-only rules (5):** Context preservation, reciprocity, data sovereignty, heritage sensitivity, language respect. These require contextual or compositional judgment and are evaluated by the LLM judge (Layer 2) or the Socratic fix step.

**What is NOT covered:** Full anthropological ethics requires understanding of specific community protocols and governance structures, per-jurisdiction data governance laws, nuanced understanding of countless cultural contexts, institutional research board (IRB) requirements, and professional judgment on complex ethical dilemmas in fieldwork and representation. This class teaches an LLM to recognize the most common anthropological ethical standards and to follow them at generation time. It does not replace a research ethics committee, institutional IRB, or community-level governance process.

## Verification

Each rule with `check_regex` is FAIL/PASS tested in `tests/test_credential_anthropology.py`. Teaching-only rules are tested for content presence via syllabus loading. A rule cannot ship without its test pair.
