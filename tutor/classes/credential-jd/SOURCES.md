# SOURCES — credential-jd (Attorney Professional Standards)

All rules in `class.yaml` derive from the ABA Model Rules of Professional
Conduct, the Restatement (Third) of the Law Governing Lawyers, and the
Federal Rules of Evidence. Every rule cites its specific source. No rule
exists without a source.

## Primary standards

### ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct (2024)
- **American Bar Association, Model Rules of Professional Conduct (2024).**
  <https://www.americanbar.org/groups/professional_responsibility/publications/model_rules_of_professional_conduct/>
- Rules cited: 1.1 (Competence), 1.4 (Communication), 1.5 (Fees),
  1.6 (Confidentiality), 1.7 (Conflict of Interest: Current Clients),
  1.9 (Conflict of Interest: Former Clients), 1.15 (Safekeeping Property),
  1.16 (Declining or Terminating Representation), 3.1 (Meritorious Claims),
  3.2 (Expediting Litigation), 3.3 (Candor Toward the Tribunal),
  3.4 (Fairness to Opposing Party and Counsel).

### Restatement (Third) of the Law Governing Lawyers
- **American Law Institute, Restatement of the Law Third, The Law Governing
  Lawyers (2000, updated 2025).**
- Sections cited: 14-16 (Attorney-Client Relationship), 59-62 (Confidential
  Client Information), 68-76 (Confidentiality and Privilege), 79-85 (Conflicts
  of Interest), 87-92 (Duties to Clients), 121-124 (Withdrawal), 127-135
  (Fee Arrangements).

### Federal Rules of Evidence
- **Federal Rules of Evidence, Article V: Privileges.**
  <https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/fre>
- FRE 501 (Privilege in General), FRE 502 (Attorney-Client Privilege and
  Work Product), FRE 611 (Mode and Order of Examining Witnesses).
- FRE 26(b)(3) — Trial Preparation Materials (Work Product Doctrine).
- *Upjohn Co. v. United States*, 449 U.S. 383 (1981) — scope of corporate
  attorney-client privilege.
- *Hickman v. Taylor*, 329 U.S. 495 (1947) — foundational work product case.

## Coverage honesty

**Checkable rules (6):** `confidentiality`, `privilege-preservation`,
`fee-prohibited-arrangements`, `prohibition-practice-disclaimer`,
`document-format`, `prohibited-representation`.

These are codifiable as regex patterns that detect specific text-based
violations: offering to disclose client confidences, forwarding privileged
materials to unauthorized parties, proposing contingent fees in prohibited
matters, claiming attorney status, generating documents with unfilled template
placeholders, and offering conflicted representation.

They are high precision but narrow — they detect specific known-bad text
patterns but do not evaluate whether the AI's overall legal reasoning is sound.

**Teaching-only rules (10):** Scope of practice, conflict of interest,
competence, client communication, candor to tribunal, zealous advocacy,
record retention, work product doctrine, withdrawal obligations, and
verification standards. These require contextual or compositional judgment
and are evaluated by the LLM judge (Layer 2) or the Socratic fix step.

**What is NOT covered:** Full legal ethics requires understanding of
per-jurisdiction variations in court rules, substantive law accuracy,
specific procedural requirements, and professional judgment on complex
ethical questions. This class teaches an LLM to recognize the most common
attorney professional standards and to follow them at generation time. It
does not certify the AI to practice law or replace a licensed attorneys
professional judgment.
