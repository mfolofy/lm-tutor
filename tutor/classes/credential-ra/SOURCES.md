# SOURCES — credential-ra (Registered Architect Professional Standards)

All rules in `class.yaml` derive from the AIA Code of Ethics and Professional
Conduct, model building codes (IBC, IRC, IFC, IECC), ADA Standards for Accessible
Design, ICC A117.1, AIA Contract Documents, NCARB Model Law, and green building
rating systems. Every rule cites the specific standard or code section it
codifies. No rule exists without a source.

### State Licensing Board Regulations
- Every US state and territory has an architectural licensing board. Common
  provisions derived from the NCARB Model Law:
  - Practice without a license is prohibited — only a registered architect may
    use the title "architect."
  - Seal requirements: original signatures on documents; electronic seals
    permitted with digital signatures.
  - Record retention: typically 5-10 years post-project.
  - Continuing education: typically 24 PDH per biennium, with HSW component.
  - Reporting obligations: licensees must report violations to the board.
- **California:** Business and Professions Code, Division 3, Chapter 3
  (Architects Practice Act).
- **New York:** Education Law Article 147 (Architecture).
- **Texas:** Texas Occupations Code, Chapter 1051 (Architects).
- **Georgia:** O.C.G.A. Title 43, Chapter 4.
- **Florida:** Florida Statutes Chapter 481 (Architecture).
- **Illinois:** Illinois Compiled Statutes, 225 ILCS 305/ (Architecture Act).

## Coverage Honesty

**Layer 1 (check_regex) rules** cover codifiable violations with high precision:
model credential claims, seal/stamp assertions, dual compensation patterns,
supplanting offers, reporting obstruction, and unauthorized contract
certifications. They detect specific known-bad text patterns but do not evaluate
whether the overall architectural reasoning is sound.

**Checkable rules (6):** `prohibition-practice-disclaimer`, `seals-and-stamps`,
`conflict-of-interest`, `ethical-bidding`, `reporting-violations`,
`unauthorized-certification`.

**Teaching-only rules (10):** Scope of practice, public safety, codes compliance,
standard of care, contract administration, construction observation, sustainable
design, record retention, accessibility, and professional development. These
require contextual or compositional judgment and are evaluated by the LLM judge
(Layer 2) or the Socratic fix step.

The honest coverage boundary for credential-ra:
- ~35% of architectural professional standards are codifiable at Layer 1
  (clear pattern violations like credential claims, seal assertions, bidding
  violations, certification overreach)
- ~65% need LLM or human judgment (code compliance analysis, standard of care,
  contract interpretation, sustainability assessment, accessibility evaluation)

**What is NOT covered:** Full architectural professional practice requires
understanding of per-jurisdiction variations in licensing laws, specific
project types, advanced structural and building science analysis, construction
contract law, construction means and methods, and professional judgment on
complex ethical questions. This class teaches an LLM to recognize the most
common architectural professional standards and to follow them at generation
time. It does not certify the AI to practice architecture or replace a
registered architect's professional judgment.
