# SOURCES — credential-hr (Human Resources Professional Standards)

All rules in `class.yaml` derive from federal employment laws, EEOC
guidelines, NLRB decisions, and HR professional standards. Every rule
cites its specific source. No rule exists without a source.

## Primary federal statutes

### FLSA — Fair Labor Standards Act (1938, as amended)
- **29 U.S.C. 201-219.** Federal minimum wage, overtime pay, recordkeeping,
  and child labor standards.
- **29 CFR Part 541** — Exempt vs. non-exempt classification (white-collar
  exemptions: executive, administrative, professional, outside sales,
  computer employee).
- Enforced by the Wage and Hour Division (WHD) of the U.S. Department of Labor.

### FMLA — Family and Medical Leave Act (1993)
- **29 U.S.C. 2601-2654.** Entitles eligible employees to 12 workweeks of
  unpaid leave per year for: birth/adoption/foster placement, serious health
  condition of employee or family member, military family leave (26 weeks for
  caregiver leave). Job restoration and health insurance continuation required.
- **29 CFR Part 825** — DOL implementing regulations.

### ADA — Americans with Disabilities Act (1990, as amended by ADAAA 2008)
- **42 U.S.C. 12101-12213.** Prohibits discrimination against qualified
  individuals with disabilities in employment (Title I), public services
  (Title II), and public accommodations (Title III).
- **29 CFR Part 1630** — EEOC implementing regulations.
- **42 U.S.C. 12111(8)** — Definition of "qualified individual."
- **42 U.S.C. 12112(b)(5)(A)** — Reasonable accommodation requirement.
- **42 U.S.C. 12111(9)** — Definition of reasonable accommodation.
- **42 U.S.C. 12111(10)** — Undue hardship defense.
- **29 CFR 1630.2(o)** — Interactive process requirement.

### Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964
- **42 U.S.C. 2000e to 2000e-17.** Prohibits employment discrimination based
  on race, color, religion, sex (including pregnancy, sexual orientation, and
  gender identity), and national origin.
- **42 U.S.C. 2000e-2** — Unlawful employment practices (disparate treatment,
  disparate impact).
- **42 U.S.C. 2000e-3** — Retaliation prohibition.
- **42 U.S.C. 2000e-5** — EEOC enforcement, charge process.
- *Bostock v. Clayton County*, 590 U.S. 644 (2020) — Title VII protects
  sexual orientation and gender identity.

### ADEA — Age Discrimination in Employment Act (1967)
- **29 U.S.C. 621-634.** Prohibits discrimination against individuals age 40
  and older. Covers employment, apprenticeship, and benefits.

### EPA — Equal Pay Act (1963)
- **29 U.S.C. 206(d).** Prohibits sex-based wage discrimination for
  substantially equal work requiring equal skill, effort, and responsibility.

### COBRA — Consolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act (1985)
- **29 U.S.C. 1161-1168.** Requires group health plans to offer continuation
  coverage for 18-36 months following qualifying events (termination, reduction
  in hours, death, divorce, Medicare eligibility).

### WARN Act — Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act (1988)
- **29 U.S.C. 2101-2109.** Requires 60 calendar days written notice for plant
  closings and mass layoffs affecting 50+ employees (or 33% of workforce at a
  single site, minimum 50).

### IRCA — Immigration Reform and Control Act (1986)
- **8 U.S.C. 1324a.** Prohibits hiring unauthorized workers. Requires I-9
  employment eligibility verification for all new hires. Anti-discrimination
  provisions (unfair immigration-related employment practices).

### NLRA — National Labor Relations Act (1935, as amended)
- **29 U.S.C. 151-169.** Protects employee rights to organize, bargain
  collectively, and engage in concerted activity. Enforced by the NLRB.
- **29 U.S.C. 157** — Section 7 rights (right to self-organize, form unions,
  engage in concerted activities).
- **29 U.S.C. 158** — Section 8 unfair labor practices.

### GINA — Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act (2008)
- **42 U.S.C. 2000ff to 2000ff-11.** Prohibits employment discrimination
  based on genetic information, including family medical history.

### USERRA — Uniformed Services Employment and Reemployment Rights Act (1994)
- **38 U.S.C. 4301-4335.** Protects civilian employment and reemployment
  rights of military service members.

## State employment laws

State employment laws may provide greater protections than federal law.
Key areas of divergence:

| Area | Federal standard | State variation examples |
|------|------------------|--------------------------|
| Minimum wage | $7.25 (FLSA) | CA $16.50, WA $16.28, NY $16.00 (2025-2026) |
| Paid sick leave | Not required by federal law | CA, NY, MA, OR, WA, CO, CT, ME, AZ, VT, MD, RI, NJ, MN, NV, IL, MI, NM (varying accrual/usage rules) |
| Paid family leave | FMLA (unpaid) | CA, NY, NJ, RI, MA, WA, CT, OR, CO, MD, DE, MN (partial wage replacement) |
| At-will employment | Default rule (most states) | MT (good cause required after probation); public policy exception in most states |
| Non-compete agreements | FTC ban (2024, partially enjoined) | CA, ND, OK (nearly banned); MA, IL, OR (income thresholds) |
| Marijuana testing | Not federally regulated | 24 states legalized recreational use; testing restrictions in CA, NY, MT, WA |
| Pay transparency | Not federally required | CA, NY, CO, WA (salary ranges in job postings); 10+ states with laws |
| AI in hiring | NYC Local Law 144 (bias audit) | IL, MD, CA pending; EEOC AI guidance (2024) |

## Coverage honesty

**Checkable rules (6):** `confidentiality`, `anti-discrimination`,
`performance-management`, `harassment-prevention`, `compensation-practices`,
`recordkeeping`.

These are codifiable as regex patterns that detect specific text-based
violations: offering to disclose employee records without authorization,
using discriminatory hiring language, suggesting termination without
proper process, minimizing harassment reports, misclassifying employees
as exempt, and recommending premature destruction of HR records.

They are high precision but narrow — they detect specific known-bad text
patterns but do not evaluate whether the AI's overall employment law
reasoning is sound.

**Teaching-only rules (10):** Scope of practice, FLSA, FMLA,
hiring practices, accommodations, termination, layoff compliance (WARN),
labor relations (NLRA), immigration compliance (IRCA), and employee
privacy. These require contextual or compositional judgment and are
evaluated by the LLM judge (Layer 2) or the Socratic fix step.

**What is NOT covered:** Full HR practice requires understanding of
per-jurisdiction employment laws, specific industry regulations, collective
bargaining agreements, and professional judgment on complex employee
relations issues. This class teaches an LLM to recognize the most common
HR professional standards and to follow them at generation time. It does
not certify the AI to practice HR or replace an experienced HR
professional's judgment.

## Verification

Each rule with `check_regex` is FAIL/PASS tested in
`tests/test_credential_hr.py`. Teaching-only rules are tested for content
presence via syllabus loading. A rule cannot ship without its test pair.
