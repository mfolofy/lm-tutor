# SOURCES — credential-pe (Professional Engineer Professional Standards)

All rules in `class.yaml` derive from the NSPE Code of Ethics, the ASCE Code of
Ethics, NCESS Model Law / Model Rules, state engineering licensure laws,
building codes (IBC, ASCE 7, ACI 318, AISC 360), environmental regulations
(NEPA, Clean Water Act), and standard AIA contract documents. Every rule cites
its specific source. No rule exists without a source.

## Primary standards

### NSPE Code of Ethics (2024)
- **National Society of Professional Engineers, NSPE Code of Ethics for Engineers
  (2024).** <https://www.nspe.org/resources/ethics/code-ethics>
- Fundamental Canons cited:
  - Canon 1: Hold paramount the safety, health, and welfare of the public.
  - Canon 2: Perform services only in areas of their competence.
  - Canon 3: Issue public statements only in an objective and truthful manner.
  - Canon 4: Act for each employer or client as faithful agents or trustees.
  - Canon 5: Avoid deceptive acts.
  - Canon 6: Conduct themselves honorably, responsibly, ethically, and lawfully
    to enhance the honor, reputation, and usefulness of the profession.
  - Canon 7: Continue their professional development throughout their careers.

### ASCE Code of Ethics (2020)
- **American Society of Civil Engineers, ASCE Code of Ethics (2020).**
  <https://www.asce.org/career-growth/ethics/code-of-ethics>
- Fundamental Principles: (1) safety, health, and welfare of the public,
  (2) service with competence, (3) integrity and impartiality,
  (4) professional development, (5) sustainable development,
  (6) maintaining the integrity of the profession.
- ASCE Canon 5: Engineers shall consider sustainable development in the
  execution of their professional duties.

### NCESS Model Law & Model Rules
- **National Council of Examiners for Engineering and Surveying, Model Law
  (2024) and Model Rules (2024).** <https://ncees.org/resources/model-law/>
- Defines the practice of engineering, exemptions, seal requirements,
  record retention, continuing education, and disciplinary procedures.
- Section 170.20: Definition of "Practice of Engineering" — any service or
  creative work requiring engineering education, training, and experience.
- Section 210.30: Seal requirements — documents must be sealed by the
  responsible engineer or supervisor.
- Section 240.20: Continuing education — 30 PDH per biennium.
- Section 290.20: Record retention — minimum 5 years after project completion.

### Building Codes & Design Standards
- **IBC 2024 (International Building Code)** — model building code adopted
  by all 50 states, governing structural, fire, and life safety.
- **ASCE 7-22 (Minimum Design Loads and Associated Criteria for Buildings and
  Other Structures)** — load combinations, wind, seismic, snow, flood.
- **ACI 318-19 (Building Code Requirements for Structural Concrete)** —
  concrete design and construction.
- **AISC 360-22 (Specification for Structural Steel Buildings)** — steel
  design and construction.
- **NFPA 101 (Life Safety Code)** — egress, fire protection, occupancy.
- **IBC Chapter 17 (Structural Tests and Special Inspections)** —
  inspection and verification requirements.

### Environmental Regulations
- **National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA, 42 USC 4321 et seq.)** —
  environmental impact assessment for federal actions.
- **Clean Water Act (33 USC 1251 et seq.)** — NPDES permitting,
  stormwater management, wetlands protection.
- **Clean Air Act (42 USC 7401 et seq.)** — air quality regulations.

### AIA Contract Documents (for reference)
- **AIA Document B101-2017** — Standard Form of Agreement Between Owner
  and Architect — defines standard of care as "the professional skill and care
  ordinarily provided by architects practicing in the same or similar locality."
- **AIA Document A201-2017** — General Conditions of the Contract for
  Construction — defines dispute resolution, claims, and performance standards.

### State Engineering Licensure Laws
- Every US state and territory has an engineering licensure board. Common
  provisions derived from the NCESS Model Law:
  - Practice without a license is a misdemeanor in most states.
  - Seal requirements: original signatures or encrypted digital signatures.
  - Record retention: typically 5-10 years post-project.
  - Continuing education: 30 PDH biennially, often with ethics requirement.
  - Reporting obligations: licensees must report violations to the board.
- Texas: Texas Engineering Practice Act (TEPA), Occupations Code Chapter 1001.
- California: Business and Professions Code, Division 3, Chapter 7 (Professional
  Engineers Act).
- New York: Education Law Article 145 (Engineering and Land Surveying).
- Georgia: O.C.G.A. Title 43, Chapter 15.

## Coverage honesty

**Checkable rules (5):** `prohibition-practice-disclaimer`, `seals-and-stamps`,
`conflict-of-interest`, `ethical-bidding`, `reporting-violations`.

These are codifiable as regex patterns that detect specific text-based
violations: an AI claiming licensure, offering to seal engineering documents,
offering services to multiple parties without disclosure, proposing to supplant
another engineer, or advising against reporting ethical/safety violations to
the board.

They are high precision but narrow — they detect specific known-bad text
patterns but do not evaluate whether the overall engineering reasoning is
sound.

**Teaching-only rules (11):** Scope of practice, public safety, competence,
code compliance, record retention, standard of care, environmental stewardship,
continuing education, whistleblower protection, deviation disclosure, and
inspection verification. These require contextual or compositional judgment
and are evaluated by the LLM judge (Layer 2) or the Socratic fix step.

**What is NOT covered:** Full engineering ethics requires understanding of
per-jurisdiction variations in licensure laws, specific project types, advanced
structural/seismic/geotechnical analysis, construction means and methods,
contract law, and professional judgment on complex ethical questions. This
class teaches an LLM to recognize the most common professional engineering
standards and to follow them at generation time. It does not certify the AI
to practice engineering or replace a licensed Professional Engineer's
professional judgment.

## Verification

Each rule with `check_regex` is FAIL/PASS tested in
`tests/test_credential_pe.py`. Teaching-only rules are tested for content
presence via syllabus loading. A rule cannot ship without its test pair.
