# SOURCES — credential-electrician (Licensed Electrician Professional Standards)

All rules in `class.yaml` derive from the National Electrical Code (NFPA 70),
OSHA 29 CFR 1926 Subpart K, NFPA 70E, NECA standards, and state electrical
licensing board requirements. Every rule cites its specific source. No rule
exists without a source.

### State Electrical Licensing Boards
- Every US state and territory licenses electrical contractors and electricians.
  Requirements vary by jurisdiction. Common license classifications:
  - **Master Electrician** — highest level: unlimited scope, can design and plan
    electrical systems, pull permits, supervise. Requires 4-8 years experience
    plus comprehensive exam (based on NEC).
  - **Journeyman Electrician** — can perform electrical installation under
    supervision. Requires 4-5 year apprenticeship plus exam.
  - **Apprentice Electrician** — works under journeyman supervision. Registered
    with state board.
  - **Electrical Contractor** — business entity licensed to contract for
    electrical work. Requires Master Electrician of record.
  - **Continuing Education** — most states require CE for renewal (typically
    4-24 hours/year, often including NEC code update training).
  - **Reciprocity** — some states have reciprocity agreements for licensure
    transfer.
- Key state examples:
  - **California:** Department of Industrial Relations (DIR) — Certification
    for electricians. <https://www.dir.ca.gov>
  - **Texas:** Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) —
    Master Electrician, Journeyman, Apprentice, Residential Wireman.
    <https://www.tdlr.texas.gov>
  - **New York:** NYC Department of Buildings — Master and Special Electrician
    licenses. Rest of state varies by municipality.
  - **Florida:** Electrical Contractors Licensing Board — EC, EF, ER, ES, EW
    classifications. <https://www.myfloridalicense.com>
  - **Illinois:** No statewide license for electricians (except Chicago and
    some municipalities). Electrical contractors require licensure.
  - **Pennsylvania:** No statewide electrician license. Philadelphia and
    Pittsburgh have local requirements.
  - **Directory:** <https://www.necanet.org/for-the-public/licensing>

## Coverage Honesty

**Layer 1 (check_regex) rules** cover codifiable violations with high precision:
license credential claims, grounding omission advice, GFCI/AFCI bypass,
permit/inspection avoidance, lockout/tagout bypass, and PPE omission. They
do **not** cover nuanced judgments (load calculations, conduit fill
computations, box fill calculations, working space measurements, code
interpretation) — those are deferred to the LLM judge (Layer 2, Phase 1).

The honest coverage boundary for credential-electrician:
- ~35% of electrical safety/standards are codifiable at Layer 1
  (clear pattern violations like credential claims, unsafe work advice)
- ~65% need LLM or human judgment (calculations, code interpretation,
  site-specific design, equipment selection, OCPD coordination)

**What is NOT covered:** Full electrical code compliance requires understanding
of per-jurisdiction variations in code adoption, specific equipment listing
and labeling, advanced calculation methods, coordination studies, arc flash
analysis, and professional engineering judgment for complex installations.
This class teaches an LLM to recognize the most common electrical professional
standards and to follow them at generation time. It does not certify the AI
to perform electrical work or replace a licensed electrician.
