# SOURCES — credential-socialworker (Social Worker Professional Standards)

All rules in `class.yaml` derive from the NASW Code of Ethics, the ASWB Model
Social Work Practice Act, the HIPAA Privacy Rule (45 CFR 164), Title IV-E of the
Social Security Act (42 USC 671), CSWE Educational Policy and Accreditation
Standards, and state licensing board regulations. Every rule cites its specific
source. No rule exists without a source.

### ASWB Model Social Work Practice Act (2023)
- **Association of Social Work Boards, Model Social Work Practice Act (2023).**
  <https://www.aswb.org/ethics/legal-resources/model-social-work-practice-act/>
- Sections cited: Scope of practice definitions, levels of licensure (BSW, MSW,
  LSW, LMSW, LCSW), supervision requirements, grounds for discipline,
  unlicensed practice prohibitions, continuing competence requirements.

### HIPAA Privacy Rule
- **Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act, 45 CFR 164 (2024).**
  <https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/index.html>
- 45 CFR 164.502 — Uses and disclosures of protected health information.
- 45 CFR 164.504 — Organizational requirements for covered entities.
- 45 CFR 164.506 — Consent for treatment, payment, and health care operations.
- 45 CFR 164.508 — Authorization for uses and disclosures (including
  psychotherapy notes under 164.508(a)(2)).
- 45 CFR 164.512 — Uses and disclosures for which authorization not required
  (public health, abuse/neglect, law enforcement, court orders).
- 45 CFR 164.514 — Other requirements (de-identification, minimum necessary).

- 45 CFR 164.528 — Accounting of disclosures.

### Title IV-E (42 USC 671)
- **Title IV-E of the Social Security Act, 42 USC 671 (2024).**
  <https://www.acf.hhs.gov/cb/title-ive>
- 42 USC 671(a) — State plan requirements for foster care and adoption
  assistance: (10) case plan and case review system; (12) standards for
  foster family homes and child care institutions; (15) health insurance;
  (16) placement in least restrictive setting; (22) transition planning for
  youth aging out.
- 42 USC 675(1) — Case plan requirements (written plan, permanency hearing,
  health records, sibling placement).
- 42 USC 675(5) — Case review system requirements.
- 42 USC 677 — John H. Chafee Foster Care Independence Program (independent
  living services, education and training vouchers).
- Title IV-E regulations (45 CFR 1355, 1356, 1357) — Program standards,
  eligibility, data reporting, and quality assurance.
- Child and Family Services Improvement and Innovation Act (P.L. 112-34) —
  Extended foster care to age 21, transition plan requirements.
- Preventing Sex Trafficking and Strengthening Families Act (P.L. 113-183) —
  Reasonable and prudent parent standard, normalcy for children in foster care.

### CSWE Educational Policy and Accreditation Standards (2022)
- **Council on Social Work Education, 2022 Educational Policy and Accreditation
  Standards (EPAS).**
  <https://www.cswe.org/accreditation/standards/2022-epas/>
- Competency 1: Demonstrate Ethical and Professional Behavior.
- Competency 2: Advance Human Rights and Social, Racial, Economic, and
  Environmental Justice.
- Competency 3: Engage Anti-Racism, Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (ADEI)
  in Practice.
- Competency 4: Engage in Practice-Informed Research and Research-Informed
  Practice.
- Competency 5: Engage in Policy Practice.
- Competency 6: Engage with Individuals, Families, Groups, Organizations, and
  Communities.
- Competency 7: Assess Individuals, Families, Groups, Organizations, and
  Communities.
- Competency 8: Intervene with Individuals, Families, Groups, Organizations,
  and Communities.
- Competency 9: Evaluate Practice with Individuals, Families, Groups,
  Organizations, and Communities.

### State Licensing Board Regulations
- All 50 states + DC regulate social work through state licensing boards
  (typically under a Board of Social Work Examiners or similar authority).
- Common regulatory themes: scope of practice per license level, supervised
  experience requirements (typically 2-3 years/3000+ hours post-MSW for
  clinical licensure), continuing education requirements (typically 20-48
  hours/biennium), ethics training mandates, jurisprudence examination
  requirements, grounds for disciplinary action (unprofessional conduct,
  impairment, fraud, criminal conviction, boundary violations), and
  mandatory reporting obligations for client harm, abuse, and impaired
  practitioners.
- States adopting the ASWB Model Practice Act (or substantially similar
  frameworks): approximately 35+ as of 2025, with increasing convergence
  on the model act's definition of social work, scope of practice, and
  disciplinary standards.

## Key Case Law

- **Tarasoff v. Regents of the University of California, 17 Cal. 3d 425
  (1976).** Duty to protect identifiable victims from credible threats of
  violence. Widely adopted across states; defines the boundary between
  confidentiality and the duty to warn/protect.
- **Jablonski v. United States, 712 F.2d 391 (9th Cir. 1983).** Duty extends
  to warning about a patient's known dangerousness even without a specific
  threat.
- **Ewing v. Goldstein, 120 Cal. App. 4th 807 (2004).** Duty extends to
  threats communicated by a family member.
- **L.L. v. Medical Protective Co., 362 Wis. 2d 604 (2014).** Social worker
  confidentiality duties under Wisconsin law; limits on duty to warn.
- **Santosky v. Kramer, 455 U.S. 745 (1982).** Standard of proof in child
  welfare termination of parental rights proceedings (clear and convincing
  evidence). Relevant to Title IV-E case planning and permanency.
- **In re Gault, 387 U.S. 1 (1967).** Due process rights in juvenile
  proceedings; relevance to adolescent clients' self-determination.
- **Olmstead v. L.C., 527 U.S. 581 (1999).** Least restrictive setting for
  individuals with disabilities; integration mandate.

## Coverage honesty

**Checkable rules (5):** `scope-of-practice`, `confidentiality`,
`informed-consent`, `dual-relationships`, `self-determination`.

These are codifiable as regex patterns that detect specific text-based
violations: claiming social worker credentials without disclosure, offering to
disclose client information without authorization, beginning services without
informed consent, proposing dual or boundary-violating relationships, and
overriding client self-determination through coercive or directive language.
They are high precision but narrow — they detect specific known-bad text
patterns but do not evaluate whether the AI's overall social work reasoning
is sound.

**Teaching-only rules (10):** Advocacy, cultural competence, mandatory
reporting, professional boundaries, documentation, termination, supervision,
ethics, competence, and social justice. These require contextual or
compositional judgment and are evaluated by the LLM judge (Layer 2) or the
Socratic fix step.

**What is NOT covered:** Full social work ethics requires understanding of per-
jurisdiction variations in mandatory reporting laws, state-specific licensing
requirements, scope differences between BSW/LSW/LMSW/LCSW credentials, nuanced
ethical dilemmas in complex multi-party situations (child welfare, custody
evaluations, organizational ethics), Title IV-E plan compliance specifics,
Medicaid billing rules, court-involved practice, and the integration of
clinical judgment with social justice values. This class teaches an LLM to
recognize the most common social work professional standards and to follow them
at generation time. It does not certify the AI to provide social work services
or replace a licensed professional's judgment.

## Verification

Each rule with `check_regex` is FAIL/PASS tested in
`tests/test_credential_socialworker.py`. Teaching-only rules are tested for
content presence via syllabus loading. A rule cannot ship without its test pair.
