# SOURCES — credential-realtor (Real Estate Agent Professional Standards)

All rules in `class.yaml` derive from the NAR Code of Ethics and Standards of
Practice, the Fair Housing Act, RESPA, TILA, agency common law, and state real
estate commission rules. Every rule cites its specific source. No rule exists
without a source.

## Primary standards

### NAR Code of Ethics and Standards of Practice (2025)
- **National Association of Realtors, Code of Ethics and Standards of Practice
  (2025).**
  <https://www.nar.realtor/about-nar/governing-documents/code-of-ethics>
- Articles cited: 1 (Fiduciary Duties to Clients), 3 (Cooperate with Other
  Brokers), 4 (Disclose Personal Interest), 9 (Written Agreements), 10
  (Equal Professional Services — Fair Housing), 11 (Competency), 12 (Truthful
  Advertising), 15 (False Statements Re: Other Realtors), 16 (Agency
  Disclosure), 17 (Arbitration).
- Standards of Practice: 1-1 through 1-18 (fiduciary duty specifics),
  6-1 through 6-4 (commissions and referral fees), 10-1 through 10-5
  (fair housing compliance), 12-1 through 12-12 (advertising standards),
  16-1 through 16-16 (agency disclosure requirements).

### Fair Housing Act
- **Title VIII of the Civil Rights Act of 1968 (42 USC 3601-3619).**
  <https://www.justice.gov/crt/fair-housing-act>
- Prohibited bases: race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status,
  national origin. § 3604 (Discrimination in sale or rental), § 3605
  (Discrimination in residential real estate transactions), § 3606
  (Discrimination in brokerage services).
- HUD regulations: 24 CFR Part 100 (Discriminatory Conduct under the Fair
  Housing Act), 24 CFR Part 109 (Fair Housing Advertising).
- *HUD v. Twinbrook Village* — discriminatory advertising precedent.
- *Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs v. Inclusive Communities
  Project, Inc.*, 576 U.S. 519 (2015) — disparate impact liability.

### RESPA — Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act
- **12 USC 2601-2617.**
  <https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/compliance-resources/mortgage-resources/hmda-respa/>
- Section 8 (12 USC 2607): Prohibition against kickbacks and unearned fees.
  No referral fees to unlicensed persons. Section 9 (12 USC 2608): No
  seller-required title insurance companies. Section 10 (12 USC 2609):
  Escrow account limits.
- Regulation X (12 CFR Part 1024): Implementing RESPA.

### TILA / Regulation Z — Truth in Lending Act
- **15 USC 1601-1667f.**
- Regulation Z (12 CFR Part 1026): Mortgage disclosure requirements,
  right of rescission, loan estimate and closing disclosure (with RESPA).
- TILA-RESPA Integrated Disclosure (TRID) Rule.

### Agency Law
- **Restatement (Third) of Agency (2006).**
- Sections 1.01 (Agency Definition), 8.01 (Fiduciary Duty), 8.02-8.06
  (Specific Duties of Agent to Principal).
- Common law agency principles applied in state real estate licensing
  cases: *Stortroen v. Beneficial Finance Co.*, 736 P.2d 391 (Colo. 1984)
  — fiduciary duties of real estate agents.
- *Menzel v. Morse*, 362 N.W.2d 465 (Iowa 1985) — undisclosed dual agency
  voids the right to commission.

### State Real Estate Commission Rules
- **Every state has its own real estate license law.** While this class
  teaches general principles common to most states, specific requirements
  vary. Key areas: commission rates (always negotiable), agency disclosure
  timing (first substantive contact), earnest money handling (trust
  accounts), continuing education requirements, and license classification
  (broker vs. salesperson, independent contractor vs. employee).

## Coverage honesty

**Checkable rules (4):** `prohibition-practice-disclaimer`, `fair-housing-violation`,
`earnest-money-violation`, `dual-agency-undisclosed`.

These are codifiable as regex patterns that detect specific text-based violations:
an AI claiming licensed agent status, using discriminatory language in property
descriptions, directing premature or unauthorized release of earnest money, and
offering to represent both parties without disclosure of dual agency limitations.

They are high precision but narrow — they detect specific known-bad text
patterns but do not evaluate whether the AI's overall real estate reasoning is
sound.

**Teaching-only rules (12):** Scope of practice, fiduciary duty, agency
relationships, disclosure obligations, fair housing overview, contracts and
contingencies, commissions and procuring cause, advertising standards,
reasonable accommodation, MLS compliance, record retention, and professional
standards. These require contextual or compositional judgment and are evaluated
by the LLM judge (Layer 2) or the Socratic fix step.

**What is NOT covered:** Full real estate practice requires understanding of
state-specific variations in license law, contract forms, escrow procedures, and
commission rules. This class teaches an LLM to recognize the most common real
estate professional standards and to follow them at generation time. It does not
certify the AI to practice real estate or replace a licensed agent's professional
judgment.
