# SOURCES — credential-vet (Veterinary Professional Standards)

All rules in `class.yaml` derive from the AVMA Principles of Veterinary
Medical Ethics, the Animal Welfare Act, DEA controlled-substances regulations,
AMDUCA, state veterinary practice acts, and VCPR requirements. Every rule
cites its specific source. No rule exists without a source.

## Primary standards

### AVMA Principles of Veterinary Medical Ethics (2024)
- **American Veterinary Medical Association, Principles of Veterinary Medical
  Ethics (2024).**
  <https://www.avma.org/resources-tools/avma-policies/principles-veterinary-medical-ethics>
- **Key provisions cited:**
  - Principle I — Veterinarians must practice with honesty, integrity,
    and compassion.
  - Principle II — Veterinarians must provide competent veterinary medical
    service and respect animal life and welfare.
  - Principle III — Veterinarians must maintain client confidentiality.
  - Principle IV — Veterinarians must report suspected cruelty, abuse, or
    neglect to appropriate authorities.
  - Principle V — Veterinarians must conduct themselves professionally in
    all interactions including fee disputes.

### Animal Welfare Act
- **Animal Welfare Act**, 7 U.S.C. § 2131–2160.
- **USDA APHIS Regulations**, 9 CFR Part 1–4.
- <https://www.aphis.usda.gov/aphis/ourfocus/animalwelfare>
- **Key provisions:**
  - § 2143 — Licensing and registration requirements.
  - § 2151 — Minimum standards for humane handling, housing, care, and
    treatment.
  - 9 CFR § 3.1–3.19 — Standards for dogs and cats (housing, exercise,
    sanitation, ventilation).
  - 9 CFR § 3.127–3.142 — Transportation standards.

### DEA Controlled Substances Regulations
- **Controlled Substances Act**, 21 U.S.C. § 801–971.
- **DEA Regulations**, 21 CFR Part 1300–1317.
- <https://www.deadiversion.usdoj.gov/>
- **Key provisions cited:**
  - 21 USC § 822 — Registration requirements.
  - 21 CFR Part 1304 — Records and reports.
  - 21 CFR Part 1306 — Prescriptions.
  - 21 CFR Part 1317 — Disposal of controlled substances.
  - DEA Form 222 — Schedule I / II order form.
  - 21 USC § 830 — Electronic prescribing requirements.

### AMDUCA — Animal Medicinal Drug Use Clarification Act
- **Animal Medicinal Drug Use Clarification Act of 1994 (AMDUCA)**,
  21 U.S.C. § 360b.
- **FDA Regulations**, 21 CFR Part 530 — Extralabel Drug Use in Animals.
- <https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/development-approval-process/animal-medicinal-drug-use-clarification-act-amduca>
- **Key provisions:**
  - § 530.10 — Conditions for permitted extralabel use (valid VCPR, no
    approved alternative, withdrawal times, recordkeeping).
  - § 530.20 — Prohibition on extralabel use in animal feed.
  - § 530.30 — Recordkeeping requirements (minimum 2 years).
  - § 530.40 — Drugs prohibited for extralabel use in food animals.

### State Veterinary Practice Acts
- **AVMA Model Veterinary Practice Act (current edition).**
  <https://www.avma.org/resources-tools/avma-policies/model-veterinary-practice-act>
- State-by-state variations in: VCPR requirements, telemedicine rules,
  record retention periods, controlled-substances oversight, mandatory
  reporting, and scope-of-practice limitations.
- Most states require annual renewal of veterinary license + continuing
  education hours.

### VCPR Requirements
- **AVMA Definition of the Veterinarian-Client-Patient Relationship (2024).**
  <https://www.avma.org/resources-tools/avma-policies/veterinarian-client-patient-relationship-vcpr>
- **FDA 21 CFR § 530.10** — Extralabel drug use requires a valid VCPR.
- **FSMB / AVMA Telemedicine Policy** — Telemedicine cannot establish a VCPR
  where one did not previously exist; in-person examination required.

## Coverage honesty

**Checkable rules (6):** `scope-of-practice`, `controlled-substances-registration`,
`informed-consent`, `mandatory-reporting-cruelty`, `confidentiality`,
`prescribing-extralabel`.

These are codifiable as regex patterns that detect specific text-based
violations: claiming veterinarian status, dispensing controlled substances
without DEA registration, proceeding without documented consent, suppressing
cruelty reports, exposing client PII, and extralabel drug use without AMDUCA
compliance.

They are high precision but narrow — they detect specific known-bad text
patterns but do not evaluate whether the AI's overall veterinary reasoning
is sound or whether all AMDUCA conditions are met.

**Teaching-only rules (9):** VCPR establishment, controlled-substances
handling/disposal, AWA housing and transport standards, euthanasia decisions,
zoonotic disease reporting, adverse drug event reporting, withdrawal times for
food animals, recordkeeping standards, professional conduct and ethics.

These require contextual or compositional judgment and are evaluated by the
LLM judge (Layer 2) or the Socratic fix step.

**What is NOT covered:** Full veterinary practice compliance requires
veterinary licensure, DEA registration, state business licenses, DEA and
state registration for controlled substances, USDA/APHIS registration for
regulated species, professional liability insurance, and hundreds of
regulatory requirements varying by practice type (small animal, large animal,
exotic, equine, food animal). This class teaches an LLM to recognize the
most common veterinary professional standards violations and to follow them
at generation time. It does not replace a compliance officer, risk manager,
or licensed veterinarian.
