# SOURCES — credential-lcsw (Licensed Clinical Social Worker / Therapist Professional Standards)

All rules in `class.yaml` derive from the NASW Code of Ethics, APA Ethical
Principles of Psychologists and Code of Conduct, ACA Code of Ethics, the HIPAA
Privacy Rule (45 CFR 164), the Tarasoff duty to protect, and state licensing
board regulations. Every rule cites its specific source. No rule exists without
a source.

## Primary standards

  <https://www.socialworkers.org/About/Ethics/Code-of-Ethics>

### HIPAA Privacy Rule
- **Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act, 45 CFR 164 (2024).**
  <https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/index.html>
- 45 CFR 164.502 — Uses and disclosures of protected health information.
- 45 CFR 164.508 — Authorization for uses and disclosures.
- 45 CFR 164.512 — Uses and disclosures for which authorization not required
  (public health, abuse/neglect, law enforcement, court orders).
- Minimum necessary standard: 45 CFR 164.502(b).
- Psychotherapy notes: 45 CFR 164.508(a)(2) — heightened protection requiring
  separate authorization.

### Key Case Law
- **Tarasoff v. Regents of the University of California, 17 Cal. 3d 425 (1976).**
  Duty to protect identifiable victims from credible threats of violence.
- **Tarasoff v. Regents, 131 Cal. Rptr. 14 (Cal. 1976)** (rehearing).
- **Jablonski v. United States, 712 F.2d 391 (9th Cir. 1983)** — duty extends
  to warning about dangerousness of a patient known to be violent.
- **Ewing v. Goldstein, 120 Cal. App. 4th 807 (2004)** — duty extends to
  threats communicated by a family member.
- **Emerich v. Philadelphia Center for Human Development, 720 A.2d 1032
  (Pa. Super. 1998)** — Tarasoff duty extends to suicidal patients.
- **Osheroff v. Chestnut Lodge, 490 A.2d 720 (Md. Ct. Spec. App. 1985)** —
  standard of care in treatment selection; relevance to evidence-based practice.

## Coverage honesty

**Checkable rules (6):** `scope-of-practice`, `confidentiality`,
`informed-consent`, `dual-relationships`, `crisis-protocol`,
`evidence-based-practice`.

These are codifiable as regex patterns that detect specific text-based
violations: claiming licensed practitioner identity, offering to disclose
client information without consent, beginning treatment without informed
consent, proposing dual relationships, promising secrecy about harm, and
guaranteeing treatment outcomes. They are high precision but narrow — they
detect specific known-bad text patterns but do not evaluate whether the AI's
overall clinical reasoning is sound.

**Teaching-only rules (9):** Mandatory reporting, professional boundaries,
cultural competence, documentation, termination, self-care, competence,
assessment ethics, and supervision/consultation. These require contextual or
compositional judgment and are evaluated by the LLM judge (Layer 2) or the
Socratic fix step.

**What is NOT covered:** Full clinical ethics requires understanding of
per-jurisdiction variations in mandatory reporting laws, state-specific
licensing requirements, scope differences between LCSW/LMFT/LPC/LPCC
credentials, nuanced diagnostic criteria, and professional judgment on complex
ethical dilemmas. This class teaches an LLM to recognize the most common mental
health professional standards and to follow them at generation time. It does
not certify the AI to provide clinical services or replace a licensed
clinician's professional judgment.

## Verification

Each rule with `check_regex` is FAIL/PASS tested in
`tests/test_credential_lcsw.py`. Teaching-only rules are tested for content
presence via syllabus loading. A rule cannot ship without its test pair.
